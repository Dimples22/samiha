Lately, and Other Things

Published Nov 14, 2026

I don't know who's going to read this, but I know some people like to lurk, some may be genuinely interested in what I have to say, and some may simply want to hate. But I've somewhat stopped caring about who's watching. I still experience a degree of paranoia about it, but the prospect of being judged is becoming less frightening to me. Perhaps it's because I spend most of my days in solitude, without speaking to many people. There's no one I feel I have to walk on eggshells around or whose opinion I feel I need to accommodate.

─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ─── ✧ ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ───

But yes, without getting too deeply into the personal aspects of my life, it's been a long journey. I've tried, and I've probably made a fool of myself or embarrassed myself along the way, but I've reached a point where I choose to be more selective about what I share. That doesn't mean I'm going to stop trying simply because a book doesn't sell many copies or because I barely have an established social media presence. Bringing it back to my earlier point, I do this for myself. I try for myself. Sometimes that means doing things unconventionally, but I'd rather do that than mould myself into something more palatable. Not because there's anything inherently wrong with trying to get your work seen by adapting to the algorithm, but because it doesn't really appeal to me. That's not to say I'd never consider it.

༺ ♡ ༻ ────────── ༺ ♡ ༻

I'm not really someone who likes to judge others, either. I think a lot of people nowadays can lack nuance when discussing certain subjects. They'll attack someone whilst justifying their own moral superiority, but it often comes across as a lack of knowledge and compassion. I don't have a problem with people holding strong opinions, but I do think meaningful debates and conversations about contentious topics deserve more thought than they sometimes receive. There's a tendency to speak with authority about things without having considered the many perspectives involved, and I think that does a disservice to the conversation itself.

✧･ﾟ: *✧･ﾟ:* ───── *:･ﾟ✧*:･ﾟ✧

Lately, I've been falling in love with writing again. I've been working on a novel and spend hours upon hours writing, refining my prose, researching, world-building and developing my characters. I want to create what one might call my magnum opus. I want to write something that stays with a reader long after they've finished reading it, something that brings meaning to their life in some way. One thing I've been working on is experimenting with different writing styles. For a blog, that means writing in a more conversational manner, whereas for my novel, I'm writing literary prose inspired by nineteenth-century novels, much like my first book, although hopefully ten times better. I think many people who create things eventually become quite critical of their earlier work, and I suppose I feel that way about my first book. Perhaps that's part of why I'm putting so much more into my second. I want to see how far I can take my writing and what I'm capable of creating when I give myself the time to develop it.

─── ⋆｡ﾟ☁︎｡⋆｡ ﾟ☾ ﾟ｡⋆ ───

I've also been doing quite well at distancing myself from certain aspects of social media, mainly Instagram. I still enjoy using TikTok and Tumblr, for the most part, but I've become more conscious of what I consume. I rarely scroll through Instagram anymore unless I want to post something. I find there's considerably more toxicity there, and I don't feel the need to expose myself to it every day. I've also made an effort to avoid doom-scrolling and instead do what I like to call hope-scrolling. Tumblr has definitely been a refuge, and I've found a community there whose posts I genuinely enjoy reading.

୨୧ ──────── ✧ ──────── ୨୧

I've noticed a considerable difference in how I perceive things, too. It's easy to get caught up in toxic advice, sweeping generalisations about people and relationships, and the constant stream of commentary about what the world is supposedly like. But I've come to realise how much perception matters. Reality isn't necessarily what some mainstream TikTok account or Instagram reel claims it to be. I refuse to let other people's assumptions dictate how I perceive my own life, especially when so much of the advice circulating online encourages people to view themselves and others through someone else's understanding of the world. Enlightenment, after all, comes from within. As within, so without. I've learnt that who I am is something I decide for myself, rather than something I derive from what social media presents as the ideal person. I decide what is ideal, and I choose to live by that.
